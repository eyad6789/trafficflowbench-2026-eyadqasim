Learning across a change of run, inside the online rule

TrafficFlowBench 2026 (IEEE Big Data Cup) — final report of team Eyad Qasim (Kaggle eyadqasim; Eyad Qasim Raheem, independent)
Selected submission sub_e50.csv: public 0.88236, private 0.87520 (rank 88 of 801).

Summary. The two scored months are not more draws of the training months: their traffic comes from independent runs, and the places where queues form moved. Most of our gains came from building every model so that it cannot memorise where the training run queued, and from validating on windows that copy how the organizers select and hide the scored windows. Task 1 uses a no-memory U-Net with time-of-day profiles taken from each scored month itself (Task 1 is offline). Task 2 uses gradient-boosted models trained on pseudo-months that reproduce the published window selector and the 90-minute blanks after each origin, with month-to-date evidence read only from rows stamped before the origin. On April the boosted ongoing model alone raised the ongoing IoU from 0.840 to 0.884. Every Task 2 feature is recomputed with all rows after the origin removed and checked to be identical before predictions are written.

2. What the data taught us

2.1 The scored months come from a different run

On the training months a U-Net reached an ongoing IoU of 0.92 on February windows built with the published selector, against 0.79 for the public model, yet on the March leaderboard every model we tried landed between 0.80 and 0.83. Input statistics were indistinguishable (adversarial validation AUC 0.47-0.54, same step noise, same 30-minute persistence), so we compared where queues form. They moved: on I5 South, links 43-45 started 38% of training onsets and none in March, while links 10-13 went from 18% to 40%; on I10 East, links 11-23 were queued 9% of the time in training and 0.1% in March; on I405 South, links 32-39 went from 18% to 38%. The month-to-month correlation of the onset map is 0.96 inside the training split but 0.44 between training and March (I5 South). The documentation later confirmed that validation and private were generated independently, with their own demand draws and incidents. Anything that memorises where the training run queued — time-of-day profiles from training months, link and corridor indices, empirical capacities — therefore misleads on the scored months.

The consequence is different for the two kinds of task. Task 1 is offline, so its profiles can come from the scored month itself. Task 2 is online, so a window may only use evidence stamped before its origin: earlier days of the same month (and, for April, the training split and all of March).

2.2 The scored windows follow a published selector, and the data around them is blanked

  • Onset windows are first feasible origins. In all 40 official June training windows the first queued cell appears at exactly T+30 (one has none, by observation noise), so only the T+30 step can be queued.
  • Windows are the earliest qualifying origins of the month, at least 6 hours apart across both conditions. Each corridor gets roughly one onset (around 09:00) and one ongoing window (around 15:00) per day, so all scored windows sit in the first 1-16 days of the month: 21% of onset windows have less than one day of month-to-date evidence, 22% less than two.
  • The 90 minutes after every origin are blank. Rows T+1 to T+18 of every scored window are missing in the released layer (0% coverage), while the 60-minute histories are released at 83% coverage against 58% for the ordinary masked layer. A later window therefore never sees how the earlier queues of the month began.

2.3 Local validation must copy 2.2, or it lies

Our first harness used calendar windows of March, read through the masked layer and outside the scored windows. It said that fine-tuning networks on earlier days of the same month lifted the ongoing IoU from 0.873 to 0.914; the leaderboard said 0.824. It also preferred expected-IoU decoding for onset by 0.05; the March leaderboard preferred a 0.55 threshold. Calendar windows are mostly mid-event, with clean evidence about the same queue, and their 58% coverage rewards models that impute well. Scored windows sit at fixed places in the month, follow blanks and carry 83% coverage. We rebuilt validation as pseudo-months: from a start day, run the selector forward, take its first five windows of each condition, blank rows T+1 to T+18 after each, and read evidence only from rows before each origin: with exact labels on February (fitted on June-December) and across the run change on March (fitted on train).

3. Task 1 (state) and Task 3 (physics)

Model. A 2-D U-Net (48-96-192-192 channels, GroupNorm, GELU, three poolings) over one-day grids of 288 steps x stations. Inputs are the released anchors as deviations from a time-of-day profile (speed, flow per lane, occupancy density per lane), ineligible-with-value channels, the profile itself, ramps, static link data, time of day, mask rate and anchor/target flags. Training uses the official train masks plus random extra anchor dropout (0-35%), so dropped anchors become extra targets; the loss is MSE on speed, MSE on flow/512, and 4 x L1 on the accumulation N = kL.

Profiles from the month itself. The profile of every month — train, March, April — is a leave-one-day-out weekday/weekend profile of that month's released cells, shrunk toward the all-days profile (κ = 6) and then the link mean. With training-run profiles the March held-out score was 0.9368 (speed RMSE 1.84 km/h); month-own profiles without retraining gave 0.9420; retraining with month-own profiles on train plus the released cells of March and April (20% of them held out, counted twice) gave 0.9487 on March and 0.9482 on April (speed RMSE 1.33, flow 29.6 vph/lane), the level reached on February before the run change. Four seeds are averaged. On the leaderboard this state alone added +0.0056 public and +0.0061 private.

Physics. A density head predicts log k; moving half of its gap to log(q/v) into the flow lowered the conservation proxy from 0.236 to 0.229 on held-out cells for a 1.4% rise in flow RMSE. Exact answers stop near 0.96 on S_physics because the flux is projected onto noisy observations.

4. Task 2 — onset

Training data. 2,373 onset events of the training split (first origins of each feasible run, labels from the unmasked layer at T+30), placed in 5,274 pseudo-month rows: for each start day (every second day) the selector picks the first five onset and ongoing windows, rows T+1..T+18 after each are blanked, histories are shown, and the evidence for window k is read from the start day up to its origin. The window order k (0-4) and the evidence span therefore match the scored months.

Features. The 83 features of the public onset model (recent speeds and flows, lags, neighbours, milepost neighbours) plus 46 evidence channels: queue rate per link over the evidence span and near this time of day on earlier days, today's rate so far, share of earlier onset events that hit the link — each earlier event read over its first six rows, since one row has only 58% coverage — the link's usual first-queue time, the share of time the link is the head of a queue (a queue grows upstream, so the head stays at the bottleneck even when the start was blanked), flows and speeds near this time on earlier days, and the same channels one to three links downstream. Two copies are trained, with and without the panel number, link index and milepost.

Models and decoding. LightGBM (learning rate 0.04, 15 leaves, 1,500 rounds) bagged over three seeds, and CatBoost (depth 7, 1,500 iterations), averaged with equal weight over both copies. For the first window of a corridor in a month there is no evidence yet, so its probability is blended 0.6 / 0.4 with the released public model. A link is predicted queued at T+30 when p ≥ 0.55; if none is, the most likely link is.

Error analysis. Of 759 March pseudo-windows, 564 are exact, 147 partial and 8.3% score zero. In the zero windows the model's best guess is usually 26 to 133 links away from the true queue and its probability on the true link averages 0.09: the queue starts at a bottleneck the month has not shown before, which is what an incident looks like (validation carries five, private seven). Partial windows are mostly a set of one or two links predicted as two or three. On I5 South the onset map changed the most and the IoU is lowest (0.55).

Decoding moved between months. On March, 0.55 beat expected-IoU by 0.024 of onset IoU; on April expected-IoU would have been 0.012 better. We chose 0.55 on March, the only month the rules allow for selecting April's method.

5. Task 2 — ongoing

Why not the networks. A 1-D U-Net over the corridor with a space-time stage reached 0.92 on February selector windows, and online fine-tuning on earlier windows of the same month looked even better on the calendar harness, but on the leaderboard the networks agreed with the public gradient-boosted models on 96% of the scored cells and scored 0.80-0.83 like them. A network sees the static channels of a corridor and can recognise where it is; that knowledge belongs to the training run.

Model. LightGBM (learning rate 0.03, 127 leaves, minimum 100 rows per leaf, feature and bagging fractions 0.7, 1,400 rounds), one row per link and horizon step. Features come from the gap-filled 60-minute history and the released row at T: speed ratios at several lags, trends and a 30-minute linear projection; flow and occupancy; neighbours one to six links away; the distance to the nearest queued link downstream and upstream now, 15 and 30 minutes ago, and their changes; the tail speed of the queue from the shock-wave relation w = (q_up - q_in)/(k_up - k_in), the time for the tail to reach each link and for a queue segment to clear, projected to every horizon step; queued-block length and position; ramps. There are no profiles, no link or corridor index, and capacity is the official value. Training rows: 8.3 million (link, step) cells of official-like windows from pseudo-months started on every day of the training split. A second model adds the 46 evidence channels of the onset model; the submission averages both and predicts queued where p ≥ 0.5 (a window with no queued cell gets its expected-IoU set).

What the selector reveals. Because the selector keeps the earliest qualifying origin at least six hours after the previous window, an origin that comes later than that boundary tells something about the horizon: the previous origin did not qualify, often because the queue would have persisted too well. In replica windows of this kind the queue at T matches the queue at T+5 with IoU 0.97 and at T+30 with 0.71 (Figure 2). We added the gap since the previous window to the features, which the rules allow, but our replica of the selector reproduces only some of the official June ongoing origins (onset origins are reproduced 4-5 out of 5 per corridor), and the feature did not help on February. We left it out.

6. Task 4 — ODME

For each corridor and split we solve min ||Af - c||² + 0.05 ||f - b||² over f ≥ 0 with the split's own released counts c and weak prior b, measured links only, by exact non-negative least squares. It reproduces the public 0.86517 pipeline's L-BFGS-B answer to a mean relative difference of 1.4e-6. Two leaderboard probes that moved the flows by a quarter (a prior-weighted solve and a multiplicative KL fit) lost 0.13 and 0.16 of S_ODME, about the distance moved, which only happens when the solution already sits on the reference.

9. The online rule

For Task 2 every input is stamped at or before the origin: the 60-minute history, the released row at T, and evidence channels computed from prefix sums that read only rows before T (earlier days of the same split; the origins of earlier windows from window_index). All models are fitted on the training split only; thresholds and blend weights were chosen on the training split and the March leaderboard. Stage 04 and stage 05 of the package repeat the organizers' check themselves: for each of the 160 scored windows of each task they set every row after the origin to missing in all arrays (speed, flow, occupancy, ramps, eligibility), recompute the window's features and stop if any value differs. All windows pass, so the predictions cannot change when those rows are removed.

Disclosure. On November 3 we scored the public model and one U-Net on April calendar windows once, as a diagnostic of the month shift (it showed the same drop as March). No component, threshold or weight of the final file was chosen from it; the decisions it could have touched (removing location memory, month-own Task 1 profiles) were already supported by the training split and March. Task 1 and Task 4 use whole months, which the rules allow for offline tasks.

10. Reproducibility

python run_all.py --data <competition input> --assets <public assets> --out run runs seven stages: s01 data preparation, s06 ODME, s04 onset, s05 ongoing, s02 four U-Net seeds (2026, 7, 11, 23), s03 state merge and s07 submission, which writes run/s07_submission/submission.csv and, given a copy of our submitted file, compares the two cell by cell. Runtime on a Kaggle machine with 4 CPUs, one T4 GPU and 30 GB RAM: about 7 hours (4.5 for the U-Nets). The stages are plain scripts and also run unchanged as Kaggle notebooks.
